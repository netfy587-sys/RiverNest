What This Guide Is All About

This website explains how to enjoy riverside stays, camping and cabins safely and responsibly. It is written for first-timers and families who want calm time near the water without surprises.

Why choose a riverside stay?

Rivers offer shade, birdsong, cooler air and a gentle background sound that many people find relaxing. They are also living, changing places. Water levels can rise and fall quickly, so a little planning makes a riverside trip safer and more enjoyable.

Choosing the right spot

Look for flat, well-drained ground well above the highest water mark. Avoid dry riverbeds and low flood plains, where water can arrive suddenly after rain upstream. Check whether camping is allowed, whether you need a permit and what facilities are available, such as drinking water and toilets.

River safety basics

Even calm-looking water can be cold and fast. Never try to cross deep or quickly moving water, keep children and pets within sight and wear a life jacket for any paddling. Check the weather for the whole river area, not just where you are, because rain far upstream can raise the level.

Camping essentials

A good tent, warm sleeping gear, a torch, a first-aid kit and a way to purify water are the basics. Untreated river water is not safe to drink. Boiling, filtering or purifying it makes it much safer. Store food in sealed containers away from your tent so wildlife is not attracted to your camp.

Leave no trace

Seven simple principles guide responsible outdoor visitors: plan ahead, use durable surfaces, dispose of waste properly, leave what you find, keep campfire impact low, respect wildlife and be considerate of others. Following them keeps riverbanks healthy and welcoming for the next visitor.

Choosing a season

Spring often brings high, fast water. Summer is warm and popular. Autumn offers beautiful colour and fewer crowds, and winter can be peaceful but cold. Each season suits a different kind of trip, so match your plans to the weather and your experience.

Quick Facts

  • Leave No Trace has seven core principles
  • Boil untreated water for at least one minute before drinking
  • Camp and use toilets about 60 metres away from the water where rules allow
  • Rain far upstream can raise a river within hours
  • Always check local rules for fires and camping

Questions People Ask

Short answers to common questions, so you can understand the basics quickly.

Is river water safe to drink?

Not without treatment. Boil it, filter it or use a purifier before drinking, even if it looks clear and clean.

Do I need permission to camp by a river?

Often yes. Rules depend on the country, park or landowner, so check before you go and use official campsites where required.

What should I do if it starts to rain heavily?

Move to higher ground early, keep away from the bank and pay attention to rising water. If in doubt, pack up and leave rather than wait.

What is the best season for a riverside trip?

It depends on your plans. Summer is warm, autumn is quiet and colourful, and spring can bring high water, so choose according to your experience.
